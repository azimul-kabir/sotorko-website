If you think you’ve been scammed

  1. Stop sending money. Hang up, leave links unopened, and keep your PIN and OTP private.
  2. If money has gone, call your provider as soon as possible using its official number below. Call 999 for urgent police, fire, or ambulance help.
  3. Keep the messages, phone numbers, and transaction screenshots. You can use them to report what happened or file a police GD.

Common scams, and what to do

"Sent by mistake, send it back"

What it looks like

A fake "Cash In" message arrives, then a call: "I sent the money by mistake, please return it."

What to do

Open your own app and check your balance first. Real money shows there. If it is not there, send nothing.

Asking for your PIN or OTP

What it looks like

Someone pretends to be from bKash, Nagad or your bank and asks for your PIN or OTP. They may ask for the code just sent to your phone.

What to do

They never ask for these. Tell no one, and hang up.

Prizes and government allowances

What it looks like

"Congratulations, you won!" or "Tap this link to get your allowance."

What to do

Do not tap unknown links. You never need a fee or PIN to receive a prize or allowance, and you never send money first.

"Your account will be blocked"

What it looks like

"Update your KYC now or your account will be blocked."

What to do

Call the official hotline yourself, not the number in the message.

A fake fine or legal notice

What it looks like

A message in the name of BRTA or the police: a vehicle fine is due, a link to pay, a short deadline and a threat of a case, sometimes a discount for paying fast.

What to do

Do not pay through the link. Open the official website yourself or ask the office. The deadline and the threat are the scammer's pressure.

Earn from home, but pay a fee first

What it looks like

A group or app promises daily income for simple tasks, then asks for an activation, verification or withdrawal fee, and then another payment.

What to do

A real platform does not charge you to withdraw your own money. Stop at the first fee; asking again is the sign.

A courier or parcel fee

What it looks like

"Your parcel is held, pay a redelivery fee", often with a link, and more money later for a "server error". It can follow an online shop that took a small advance.

What to do

Call the courier on its official number. Do not pay repeated charges; the parcel may be only an excuse.

A call or message from the police or 999

What it looks like

Someone says a case has been filed against you, then asks for money, your PIN or bank details, or tells you to call a number.

What to do

999 and the police never ask for a PIN or bank details. Hang up and call 999 or your station yourself. The number on the screen can be faked.

An accident about someone you know

What it looks like

A call or chat says your relative has had an accident or is in trouble and you must send money by bKash or Nagad now, sometimes with a copied voice or a hacked account.

What to do

Before you send anything, call that person on a number you already have, or another relative.

FAQ

Does Sotorko read my SMS?

It never asks for SMS, call log or contacts permission. Normally only the message you share or paste is checked. If you turn on automatic checks in Settings, Sotorko also reads the notifications of new messages from SMS, WhatsApp, Messenger, imo, Viber and Telegram, checks them on your phone and keeps nothing. It warns you only when a message has red flags.

Does "no warning signs found" mean the message is safe?

No. Sotorko looks for known scam patterns. A new kind of scam, or one worded differently, can slip past. If in doubt, call the hotline.

How does Sotorko know what a scam looks like?

Its rules follow scam patterns described by bKash, the government's cyber security team (BGD e-GOV CIRT), the police and news reports. The checking happens on your phone, and the app learns nothing from your messages. New kinds of scams reach it only through app updates, so it can miss new or unusual ones.

How fast does my family guardian hear?

Usually within seconds, even when the app is closed, because a push notification is sent to the guardian's phone. If a phone blocks background messages (a battery saver, for example), it can take about 15 minutes or more. The notification never contains the message text. Repeat message alerts are skipped for 24 hours after delivery, and each guardian link accepts one alert per minute.

Does my message go online?

No. Only if you join "Family guardian" with your consent, and a message is found dangerous, that message goes to the guardian you chose.

Does Sotorko file the GD for me?

No. An online GD is filed in the police's official Online GD system, with your own national ID, a live photo and an OTP. Sotorko writes what to say and shows you the steps.

Is the GD application in the police's official format?

Yes, the letter follows the official generic GD format used across Dhaka Metropolitan Police and Bangladesh Police stations: date, Officer in Charge, parents' names, national ID number, present and permanent addresses, incident facts, and prayer for record. If your station has its own printed form, the draft helps you fill it in accurately.

Are numbers being called "scammers"?

No. What others report is shown only as "reports", unverified claims. No verdict is given about anyone.

Is Sotorko connected to bKash or Nagad?

No. Sotorko is an independent app. bKash, Nagad and other names belong to their owners.
